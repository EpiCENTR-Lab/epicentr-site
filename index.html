Epithelial Cell Biology in ENT Research

Based at the UCL Great Ormond Street Institute of Child Health, the EpiCENTR group studies how airway epithelial stem cells build, maintain, and repair the respiratory epithelium.

Research

Stem Cell Biology and Ageing

We study how airway epithelial stem cells (basal cells) maintain and repair the respiratory epithelium during tissue homeostasis and ageing. Our work focuses on the molecular mechanisms that regulate stem cell behaviour, including interactions with the extracellular matrix.

We aim to define how age-associated changes in epithelial stem cells contribute to impaired regeneration and chronic lung disease. Specifically, we are working on how hemidesmosomes and transcription factors such as p63 control airway stem cell identity, function and ageing.

We have also developed lentiviral tools to study airway epithelial differentiation and identified Wnt signalling as a key regulator of basal cell proliferation and fate.

Regenerative Therapies

We also investigate cell- and gene-based approaches to repairing or replacing damaged airway epithelium, with a view to progressing these to clinic for children with airway diseases.

We have developed a new approach for the expansion of epithelial basal cells ex vivo, enabling both mechanistic studies and therapeutic development. As an initial application, we have focused on epidermolysis bullosa, where we demonstrated proof-of-concept for a combined cell and gene therapy approach.

Our ongoing work focuses on how epithelial cells can be expanded, genetically modified and delivered safely and effectively, including optimizing lentiviral transduction and surgical delivery of airway basal cells.

Opportunities

We are interested to hear from prospective postdoctoral fellows, PhD students and MRes/MSc students who wish to work on airway epithelial stem cell biology, epithelial regeneration, or cell and gene therapy for airway disease. Below are some of the funding schemes that current and former lab members have used to join the group, alongside general routes to enquire about a project. Eligibility criteria and deadlines for external fellowships are set by the funders and can change year to year, so please check the linked scheme pages for the current call before applying.

Postdoctoral fellowships

MSCA Postdoctoral Fellowships

Funded through Horizon Europe, MSCA Postdoctoral Fellowships support researchers holding a PhD (including those who have recently submitted their thesis) to develop an independent research proposal with a host lab. European Fellowships run for 1–2 years; Global Fellowships combine an outgoing phase outside Europe with a mandatory return phase. Applicants must generally not have resided in the UK for more than 12 months in the three years before the call deadline. We are happy to discuss a proposal built around airway basal cell biology, organoid models, or epithelial cell and gene therapy, and to act as host lab for an application.

MSCA scheme details & deadlines

ERS Long-Term Research Fellowships

European Respiratory Society Long-Term Research Fellowships support early-career researchers and clinicians to carry out a basic, translational or clinical research project of 6–24 months at a host institution outside their home country. These are well suited to postdoctoral researchers or clinical fellows wishing to gain experience in human airway epithelial culture, organoid models, or cell and gene therapy approaches to airway disease.

ERS LTRF scheme details & deadlines

ERS Short-Term Research Fellowships

ERS Short-Term Research Fellowships fund shorter visits of 1–3 months for early-career researchers and clinicians to learn techniques not available at their home institution. These can be a useful way to gain hands-on experience of our basal cell expansion and airway organoid methods before committing to a longer fellowship or PhD.

ERS STRF scheme details & deadlines

PhD opportunities

UCL GOS ICH Child Health Research PhD studentships

UCL Great Ormond Street Institute of Child Health runs an annual, competitive scheme of three-year, fully funded PhD studentships covering UK tuition fees, a tax-free stipend and a consumables budget. Studentships are usually advertised from around October each year, with a project portfolio published alongside the call; applicants are expected to contact their preferred project supervisor before applying. We regularly propose projects on airway epithelial stem cell biology and cell and gene therapy through this scheme – do get in touch if one of our projects is listed, or to discuss possibilities ahead of the next call.

Child Health PhD studentship scheme

UCL–Birkbeck MRC Doctoral Training Partnership

The UCL–Birkbeck MRC Doctoral Training Partnership funds four-year PhD studentships across four research themes, with rotation projects in the first year before students settle on a supervisor and thesis project. Applicants apply to a theme rather than directly to a lab, so we would encourage anyone interested in joining the group through this route to contact us in advance of choosing a rotation project.

UCL–Birkbeck MRC DTP

MRes opportunities

Child Health MRes

The UCL GOS Institute of Child Health’s Child Health MRes is a one-year (or two-year part-time) taught Master’s built around a substantial research project, with optional modules including Stem Cells and Tissue Repair. Students can choose a project from the department’s portfolio or propose their own; we are happy to discuss an MRes project on airway epithelial biology or regeneration.

Child Health MRes

Human Tissue Repair MRes

Run jointly by UCL’s Divisions of Medicine and Surgery & Interventional Science, the Human Tissue Repair MRes combines core training in inflammation, immunology and tissue repair with a laboratory-based research project from the second term onwards, following either an Immunotherapy or Applied Tissue Engineering route. We would be happy to supervise a project relevant to airway epithelial regeneration.

Human Tissue Repair MRes

Other routes

If you are self-funded, hold external sponsorship, or are considering a rotation or research project through a different MSc, MRes or PhD programme, we are still happy to discuss a project. Please get in touch by email with a CV and a short note on your research interests.
